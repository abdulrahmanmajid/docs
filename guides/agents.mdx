An agent is the AI that talks to your customers. You can have as many as you like: one for the front desk, one for sales follow-ups, one for each branch. The same agent can answer phone calls, WhatsApp, Instagram, Messenger and your website chat.

Open **Agents** to see them all. Click **Create agent ▾** to make a new one, start from a template, or duplicate one you already have.

## The six steps

Every agent is built in the same six steps. A live phone preview beside the editor shows how it will greet people.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Identity">
    **Name, face, language and personality.**

    - **Face.** Choose from a set of illustrated faces. Your team sees it everywhere the agent appears.
    - **Language.** Around 50 languages. Languages with dialects open up: Arabic, for example, offers Emirati, Saudi, Qatari, Kuwaiti, Egyptian, Jordanian, Lebanese, Iraqi, Moroccan and more. There's also an option for callers who could speak any language.
    - **Personality.** Four styles, each with a sample opening line you can hear:

      | Personality | Sounds like |
      | --- | --- |
      | **Warm** | Friendly and caring. Good for clinics and salons. |
      | **Professional** | Clear and efficient. Good for law firms and brokers. |
      | **Upbeat** | Bright and energetic. Good for shops and gyms. |
      | **Calm** | Unhurried and reassuring. Good for support lines. |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Voice">
    **How it sounds.** Browse the voice library and hover over any voice to hear it.

    Want your own voice, or your manager's? Open the voice lab and **clone** one: record a short sample or upload a clip, and Kallabot makes a voice from it. Only clone a voice you have permission to use.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Instructions">
    **What it does and how.** Write the agent's job in plain sentences: who you are, what callers usually want, what it should and shouldn't do, and when to hand over to a person. Set the **greeting**, the first line callers hear. If the agent makes calls, set a separate opening line for those calls.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Knowledge">
    **What it knows.** Attach your website, price list, files or a listings sheet. See [Knowledge](/guides/knowledge).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Tools">
    **What it can do.** Book on Google Calendar, save to Google Sheets, send a WhatsApp or email, search the web, transfer to a person, or call your own systems. See [Tools](/guides/tools).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Channels">
    **Where it works.** Pick its phone number, WhatsApp number, Instagram or Facebook Page, and web chat widget. See the channel guides under **Phone** and **Chat channels**.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Writing good instructions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Write like you'd brief a new hire" icon="pen-line">
    Short sentences, one rule each. "We close at 6pm on Thursdays" beats a paragraph about opening hours.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Say what to do when it doesn't know" icon="circle-help">
    For example: "If you don't know the price, say a colleague will call back today, and take their name and number."
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Name the hand-over moments" icon="hand">
    "If the caller is upset, or asks for the manager, offer to transfer them to reception."
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Put facts in knowledge, not instructions" icon="book-open">
    Prices, addresses and policies change. Keep them in [Knowledge](/guides/knowledge), so you update them in one place.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How it sounds human

You don't have to set any of this up. Every agent:

- Waits until the caller has actually finished, so it doesn't jump in while someone reads out a phone number.
- Lets callers interrupt, but doesn't stop for a quick "mm-hm".
- Says small "mm-hm"s while the caller talks for a while, so the line never feels dead.
- Follows callers who switch between English, Arabic, Urdu and Hindi mid-sentence.
- Ends the call politely once the conversation is clearly over.
- Never claims to be a human when asked directly.

## Publishing and versions

Your agent has two states: what callers hear (the **published** version), and what you're working on (the **draft**).

<Steps>
  <Step title="Edit freely">
    Every change saves to the draft. Callers don't hear it yet.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Test the draft">
    The **Test** tab lets you talk to your latest changes in the browser before anyone else hears them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Publish">
    Click **Publish changes**. New calls and chats use it straight away. Calls already in progress finish on the version they started with.
  </Step>
</Steps>

Every published version is kept. If a change goes wrong, open the agent's version history and restore an earlier one.

## Test your agent

Open the agent and choose **Test**, then **Start test call**. You talk to the agent through your computer's microphone, exactly as a caller would hear it. Test calls appear in **Calls** with a recording and transcript, so you can read back what happened.

To test a WhatsApp or chat agent, use the chat playground in its studio.

## Duplicate or archive

- **Duplicate an agent** to start a new one from a copy, for example the same front desk for a second branch.
- **Archive** an agent you no longer use. It stops answering, and its past calls and chats stay.
